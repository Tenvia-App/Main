Proposed early-development plan

Build evidence before trust.

A useful proof of concept should show where the idea works, where it fails, and what must be true before anyone considers field use. These are proposed tests, not completed results.

01 /

Verify offline behavior

Device and model checks

Run the app in airplane mode and confirm the intended model and core interaction remain available without a network connection.

RecordStartup and response time, battery use, stability, and any features that quietly depend on connectivity.
02 /

Run controlled emergency simulations

Never live emergencies

Use scripted, facilitator-led scenarios such as getting disoriented, an injury, or losing shelter. Observe how the assistant handles incomplete details, asks follow-up questions, signals uncertainty, and points people toward real help when appropriate.

RecordScenario protocol, response quality, unsafe or confusing outputs, and the moments a human should take over.
03 /

Probe safety boundaries

Expert review

Have qualified safety, medical, and outdoor professionals review test cases and failures. Deliberately test ambiguity, conflicting information, overconfidence, and requests the system should not answer.

RecordFailure modes, escalation rules, stop conditions, and changes required before another test round.
04 /

Study comprehension and reliance

Non-emergency participants

Invite participants into clearly labeled role-play. Check whether they understand the assistant, can correct it in their own words, and know when not to rely on it.

RecordComprehension, accessibility barriers, trust calibration, participant feedback, and points of confusion.

What partners can evaluate

A bounded pilot can produce a test protocol, evidence log, reviewed failure cases, and go / no-go criteria. That gives potential testers and public-sector partners something concrete to assess, without claiming the system is already proven safe or effective.
